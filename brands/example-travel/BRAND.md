# Wanderly (example brand, travel niche)

The worked example every new brand is modelled on. It is the travel app these factories were first built for, with names changed.

## Product and promise
Creators host group trips; travellers book a seat in the app. Promise: travel with someone you already follow.

## Audience and pain
Young office workers. Pain words that worked as one-per-beat reel hooks: Monday, deadlines, traffic, EMI, overthinking, burnout, group-chat plans that never happen.
Payoff pattern: pain words on black, then the place at the song's drop ("Cure: Spiti").

## Voice
Short, dry, relatable. Original lines only, never a reference's words. Story reel: setup line, punchline on the cut.

## Claims and compliance
Public posts are promo-only: no prices, no "book now", no sign-up links. Agency slides end "Follow {name}", never "message us". Real names on app screens (chat, profile, headcount) are never shown.

## Visual world
One art style per place so the grid reads as a set (warm screen-print for one region, cool blue for snow). Real app screens go in a tilted phone frame on key slides.

## Learned rules (from the first 30 reels)
- Stock + dark gradient + white text was rejected as "bland"; feeling comes from an illustrated scene per slide.
- Fonts were the only thing corrected on batch 1; cuts, songs, shots and captions held. Lock fonts in `style.json` early.
- No silent copy: every reel file carries its song, numbered permanently.
- Drone holds (1-3 long aerials, tiny tag) were the top performers: least editing, most reach.
- Gemini garbles any text in a picture: scenes say "blank paper", "no signs with words".
- Image spend: one seed per scene, a second only for a hero shot (about $13 for 60 images in one session).
